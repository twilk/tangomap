# Tango Map — Evidence-Gated Roadmap

Synthesised 2026-08-07 from five independent executive reviews (Zuckerberg, Bezos/Jassy,
Cook, Sarandos/Peters, Pichai), seventeen independently-checked evidence claims, and three
chair rulings.

Gated by **evidence**, not by time. There are no quarters and no dates anywhere in this
document. A gate opens when a named signal appears in our own data, and it is abandoned
when a named signal says it never will.

---

## 0. What died on evidence

**Backlog items dropped because their evidence claim came back REFUTED: zero.**

All seventeen claims across the five backlogs were checked by independent verifiers and all
seventeen came back **CONFIRMED**. Not one persona overstated the repo. That is worth
stating plainly, because it means every disagreement below is a disagreement about
*judgement*, not about facts, and none of it can be settled by another grep.

One thing did die on evidence, and it was not a persona's item — it was the **consensus
list's own item 4 rationale**:

> "Invite loop via `/compare?a=<handle>` — the loop exists in code but never fires
> *because nobody is prompted*."

CONFIRMED counter-claim: `app/compare/page.tsx:167` already renders
`<CopyButton … label="Copy invite link" />` inside the exactly-one-profile-loaded branch,
and four other surfaces (`app/me`, `app/u/[handle]`, `app/u/[handle]/card`,
`PartnerMatches.tsx`) link into `/compare`. The prompt exists. The diagnosis was wrong, and
because the diagnosis determined the build, the item had to be rewritten rather than
merely re-ranked. See §4, Gate 3.

The lesson the chair attached to that finding is adopted as policy here:

> **Every future backlog item that asserts "X does not exist" or "X never fires" carries a
> file path and a line number, or it does not go on the list.**

## 1. The definition of "complete" — one, not five

The five definitions genuinely conflict. They are not five phrasings of one goal; they name
three different bottlenecks and two different users. Blending them would produce a target
nobody can fail.

| Persona | User | "Complete" means |
|---|---|---|
| Zuckerberg | **Ola**, invited by WhatsApp | Tap invite link → appear as participant *b* in a filled compare view in under 2 min, with `link_open → signin → handle_claimed → compare_filled` all recorded |
| Bezos | **Ola**, an existing active student at ~21/62 | ≥3 of the 5 currently-active users mark ≥1 new skill in each of four consecutive weekly rollups, measured by skills-marked / active-user / week |
| Cook | **Marta**, no Google account | Signs in without Google and marks her first skill in the same session, recorded first-party, with nothing she touched less finished than the map |
| Sarandos | **Zofia**, lapsed at ~20/62 | ≥3 of 5 active members return in a given week *without in-person prompting*, and we can name the exact skill each lapsed member stopped at |
| Pichai | **Marta**, never heard of us | ≥50 of 62 `/skill/` URLs indexed in Search Console **and** ≥1 organic session continuing from a skill page into the map |

### Ruling: **Bezos's definition wins.**

> **COMPLETE = at least 3 of the 5 currently-active users mark one or more new skills in
> each of four consecutive weekly rollups, measured by skills-marked per active user per
> week.**

**Why it wins.**

1. It is the only definition that measures whether the *product* works, as opposed to
   whether a *channel* works. Pichai's criterion can be fully satisfied by Googlebot with
   zero dancers; Zuckerberg's can be satisfied by one enthusiastic friend; Cook's can be
   satisfied by a single session that never repeats. Bezos's cannot be satisfied by
   anything except people using the thing repeatedly.
2. It names an **input metric** the single developer controls, not an output metric he can
   only watch. "7 users" is weather. "Skills marked per active user per week" is a dial.
3. Every other definition is downstream of it. Sarandos's return trigger is judged on it.
   Zuckerberg's loop multiplies it. Pichai's search traffic feeds it. Cook's second auth
   path widens the funnel into it. If it is flat, every one of the others is pouring water
   into a bucket whose leak rate is unknown.

**What I am giving up by choosing it, stated honestly.** Bezos's definition is bounded to
five people the founder already knows. It cannot detect a stranger, and satisfying it
proves the product works *for a tango school*, not *as a product*. That is a real ceiling
and it is why Gate 2 exists: the definition of complete is Bezos's, but the definition of
*next* is Pichai's.

**The competing definition that came closest: Pichai's**, and it loses on a single point.
Indexed pages are a crawler's output metric wearing a product metric's clothes. Fifty
indexed URLs with a flat input metric is a failure that reads as a win, and that is exactly
the failure mode this repo already has a history of — world-class execution on an unchosen
strategy.

**Explicitly rejected as the definition:** Zuckerberg's, because its numerator is
approximately zero (7 users, 3 profiles, 2 public — the addressable sender pool for an
invite link is two people); and Cook's, because no measurement anywhere shows sign-in
abandonment, so it optimises a step nobody has observed failing.

## 3. Gate 1 — **Make it observable, and open the front door**  ⟵ NEAREST GATE

**Entry condition:** none. This is the origin gate; nothing gates it. Every other gate's
entry condition is a query against the table this gate creates, which is the whole argument
for its position.

**Abandon condition:** if, after the five calls (item 3) and the PR/FAQ (item 4), the honest
written answer is *"this is a personal tool for one tango circle, not a product"* — abandon
this roadmap entirely and keep shipping for pleasure. That is a legitimate and possibly
correct outcome, it costs zero developer-days to discover, and it is the only outcome none
of the five executives considered. Items 1 and 2 are still worth shipping in that world;
Gates 2–6 are not.

**Gate 1 ships no new user-facing surface.** One invisible table, two invisible files, one
`<script type="application/ld+json">`, two conversations. Cook's "shipping fewer things,
finished" applied to the roadmap itself.

Items 3 and 4 consume zero developer time and run in parallel with items 1 and 2. There is
a deliberately empty fifth slot; the panel's most repeated finding was that this repo builds
faster than it decides, and an empty slot is the cheapest available antidote.

### Item 1 — Instrumentation: the `event` table

**Why first.** It is the only item on all five backlogs *and* a hard precondition for the
entry condition of every later gate, including the entry condition of the item its own
sponsor ranked first. Zuckerberg proposes to fix a step (handle claim) he has not measured;
Sarandos's trigger is judged on a metric that does not exist; Pichai's success criterion
requires an organic-session record. All three are downstream of this.

**First-party only.** No PostHog, no Plausible, no gtag, no `@vercel/analytics`. Cook's
constraint is adopted as a hard rule: nothing leaves our infrastructure, no third-party
script, no IP addresses stored, no PII in event properties, no cross-site identifiers.
Adopted because it is also the frugal choice — one table in a Postgres we already pay for.

#### 1a. Schema — `db/schema.ts`

```ts
export const events = pgTable('event', {
  id: text('id').primaryKey().$defaultFn(() => crypto.randomUUID()),
  ts: timestamp('ts', { mode: 'date' }).notNull().defaultNow(),
  // Nullable: signed-out events (link_open, signin attempts) have no user yet.
  userId: text('userId').references(() => users.id, { onDelete: 'cascade' }),
  // First-party random id in an httpOnly cookie. Correlates a signed-out
  // link_open with the signin that follows it. Not a fingerprint, not shared
  // across sites, no IP, no user agent.
  anonId: text('anonId'),
  name: text('name').notNull(),
  // Skill slug for skill_mastered / skill_unmastered. Null for funnel events.
  slug: text('slug'),
  // Small, allowlisted, non-PII. e.g. { src: 'organic' }.
  props: jsonb('props').$type<Record<string, string | number | boolean>>(),
}, (e) => [
  index('event_name_ts_idx').on(e.name, e.ts),
  index('event_user_ts_idx').on(e.userId, e.ts),
  index('event_anon_ts_idx').on(e.anonId, e.ts),
]);
```

**The chair's binding rider is in this schema, not bolted on later.** `progress.mastered` is
a `jsonb` string array with **one** `updatedAt` for the whole set, and `progress_history` is
a day-granularity snapshot keyed `(userId, day)` holding 10 rows total. Per-skill mastery
timing does not exist and cannot be reconstructed. `skill_mastered` / `skill_unmastered`
rows with a `slug` and a `ts` are what make "where did they stall" a SQL query later instead
of a schema migration later. One column set, added while the table is being designed anyway.

#### 1b. The eight event names — closed allowlist

| name | emitted where | carries |
|---|---|---|
| `skill_mastered` | `app/api/progress/route.ts` PUT, diffing `mastered` against `existing` | `slug` |
| `skill_unmastered` | same place, same diff | `slug` |
| `link_open` | `app/compare/page.tsx` when `sp.a` is present | `props.a_present` |
| `signin` | Auth.js `events.signIn` callback in `auth.ts` | — |
| `handle_claimed` | `app/api/profile/route.ts` PUT, on the transition `handle: null → set` | — |
| `compare_filled` | `app/compare/page.tsx` when both `pa && pb` resolve | — |
| `invite_copied` | `CopyButton` on `/compare`, client → `POST /api/events` |— |
| `skill_page_view` | `app/skill/[slug]/page.tsx` | `slug`, `props.src` |

Zuckerberg's five-event k-factor funnel is `link_open → signin → handle_claimed →
compare_filled` plus `invite_copied`. It is included here at near-zero marginal cost *even
though his item is demoted to Gate 3*, because Gate 3's entry condition is a query over
these rows. Shipping the loop before the events would forfeit the only evidence that could
settle the argument he lost.

#### 1c. Write path

`app/api/progress/route.ts` already loads `existing` at line 42 before the upsert — the diff
is free there. Emit the mastery events in the **same best-effort `try/catch` shape** the
`progress_history` snapshot already uses at lines 60–66: telemetry must never fail a save,
but a failure must leave a `console.error` trace.

`POST /api/events` for the two client-emitted names (`invite_copied`, `skill_page_view`):
allowlist on `name`, reject unknown names with 400, no arbitrary `props` keys, rate-limit
per session/anon cookie. It is a pipe, not an API.

#### 1d. Activation and the input metric — both derived, nothing stored

Activation is **not** a stored event. It is `MIN(ts) WHERE name='skill_mastered' GROUP BY
"userId"` — one query, nothing to keep in sync, nothing to backfill wrong.

The input metric, which is the definition of complete in §1:

```sql
SELECT date_trunc('week', ts) AS wk,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE name = 'skill_mastered')                     AS marks,
       count(DISTINCT "userId") FILTER (WHERE name = 'skill_mastered')     AS active_users,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE name = 'skill_mastered')::float
         / NULLIF(count(DISTINCT "userId") FILTER (WHERE name = 'skill_mastered'), 0)
                                                                           AS marks_per_active_user
FROM "event"
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1 DESC;
```

Commit this as `docs/superpowers/queries/input-metric.sql`. Not a dashboard. Not a funnel
product. A file with a query in it, run by hand.

#### 1e. Migration discipline — non-negotiable

This repo has already taken a production outage from a schema change merged without the
migration reaching the PROD database (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-25-…`, and the
`migration-prod-target-outage` note). Next migration index is `0005`.

1. Generate `drizzle/0005_*.sql`.
2. Apply it to the **production** Supabase DB (eu-west-3).
3. Verify the `event` table and its three indexes exist **in production** with `\d event`.
4. Only then merge the branch carrying the `schema.ts` change.

**Confirm the columns in prod before merging. Not after.**

#### 1f. Tests

- `event` name allowlist is exhaustive: a unit test enumerates the eight names and asserts
  `POST /api/events` 400s on anything else.
- Mastery diffing: given `existing.mastered = [a,b]` and incoming `[a,c]`, exactly one
  `skill_unmastered(b)` and one `skill_mastered(c)` are emitted.
- Telemetry failure does not fail the save: mock the event insert to throw, assert
  `PUT /api/progress` still returns 200 with the written progress.
- No PII: a test asserts `props` is rejected if it contains keys outside the allowlist.

### Item 2 — The front door: sitemap, robots, JSON-LD, llms.txt, Search Console

**Why it is in the nearest gate despite not serving the chosen definition of complete.**
Stated openly, because this is the roadmap's one deliberate inconsistency: indexing has
latency measured in weeks, and it is the only item here whose payoff clock starts when it
ships rather than when it is measured. It is admitted as a **latency hedge**, not as
retention work, and it carries its own abandon condition at Gate 2. It is also roughly one
day against the largest unexploited asset in the repo: 62 server-rendered, statically
generated skill pages that no search engine and no AI assistant can currently find.

Verified: `app/` contains `manifest.ts` and **no** `sitemap.ts`, **no** `robots.ts`; a
repo-wide grep for `application/ld+json` returns zero matches; production serves neither
`/sitemap.xml` nor `/robots.txt`.

**Files:**

- **`src/lib/site.ts`** — first, because three of the four files below need the origin and
  it is currently a bare literal at `app/compare/page.tsx:25`
  (`const SITE = 'https://partykamap.vercel.app'`). Export `SITE_URL`, replace the literal,
  set `metadataBase` in `app/layout.tsx` so every absolute URL resolves from one place.
- **`app/sitemap.ts`** — `MetadataRoute.Sitemap` mapping `SKILLS` (the same array
  `generateStaticParams()` already iterates at `app/skill/[slug]/page.tsx:15`) plus `/`,
  `/skills`, `/compare`. **`/u/<handle>` profiles are deliberately excluded** even for
  public profiles: two rows of opt-in personal data do not belong in a crawl index, and
  including them would make the sitemap a live DB read.
- **`app/robots.ts`** — allow `/`, `/skills`, `/skill/*`, `/compare`; disallow `/me`,
  `/settings`, `/signin`, `/signout`, `/api/*`, `/u/*`. Explicitly allow the AI crawlers by
  name (`GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `Claude-Web`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended`) — Pichai's
  point that being citable by assistants is a distribution channel equal to search is
  accepted. Point `sitemap:` at `${SITE_URL}/sitemap.xml`.
- **JSON-LD in `app/skill/[slug]/page.tsx`** — one `<script type="application/ld+json">`
  emitting `LearningResource` (`name`, `description` reusing the string `generateMetadata`
  already builds, `educationalLevel: skill.level`, `teaches`, `url`, `isPartOf` the map) and
  a `BreadcrumbList` (`/` → `/skills` → this skill). Serialise with `JSON.stringify` and
  escape `<` before injection.
- **`app/llms.txt/route.ts`** — a route handler, not a static `public/llms.txt`, so it is
  generated from `SKILLS` and **cannot go stale**. Same discipline as the design-token
  staleness test this repo already has.
- **Search Console** — register the production property the same day, via
  `metadata.verification.google` in `app/layout.tsx`. Without it, Gate 2's entry condition
  is unmeasurable.

**Tests** (matching this repo's existing staleness-test culture):

- Sitemap completeness: every slug in `SKILLS` appears in `sitemap()`. Adding a 63rd skill
  without it appearing must fail CI.
- Robots: `/skill/` allowed, `/settings` and `/api/` disallowed, sitemap line present.
- JSON-LD: the emitted string parses as JSON and carries `@type`, `name`, `url` for a
  sample slug.
- `llms.txt`: returns `text/plain` and lists all 62 skill URLs.

## 4. Later gates — intent only

Rule 7 says a full spec for an unlocked gate is work for the bin. **I agree, without
reservation.** Half of this document's evidence base is a demonstration of the cost of
specifying before measuring: the consensus list's item 4 was specified in detail, survived
four executives, and was refuted by one `grep` — because it was written before anyone
looked. Each gate below gets its entry condition, its abandon condition, and one paragraph
of intent. The spec gets written when the gate opens, against the data that opened it.

### Gate 2 — Convert the strangers

**Entry:** Search Console reports ≥20 of the 62 `/skill/` URLs indexed **and** the `event`
table contains ≥1 `skill_page_view` from an organic-search session.
**Abandon:** once ≥50 URLs are indexed, Search Console shows <100 total impressions across
all 62 skill URLs — the corpus has no search demand, and every acquisition bet downstream
of it is dropped with it.

Search traffic lands deep, on `/skill/sacada`, not on the homepage — and today that page's
map CTA is a bare `<Link href="/">` at line 201 that drops the visitor's context on the
floor, because `app/page.tsx`'s `HomePage()` takes no `searchParams` at all. Item 5 adds a
`?skill=<slug>` parameter that centres and highlights that node, and points every skill
page's CTA at it; the corpus and the product stop being two disconnected graphs. Item 6
fixes whatever the five calls named as the stall, now confirmed or refuted by real
per-skill timestamps. Note the sequencing that put item 5 here rather than in Gate 1:
indexing latency gives us weeks of warning before the first stranger arrives, so the deep
link is not latency-bound and building it before there is traffic would repeat the exact
error this panel keeps flagging.

### Gate 3 — Repair the invitee side of the loop

**Entry:** ≥5 `link_open` events from sessions with no `userId`, i.e. the loop demonstrably
has senders. Plus the funnel query naming which step drops: if `signin → handle_claimed`,
build items 7 + 8; if `link_open → signin`, build items 7 + 9 instead.
**Abandon:** fewer than 3 `invite_copied` events across all users after the input metric has
been positive for four consecutive rollups — nobody wants to invite anyone. Delete the
loop, remove the `CopyButton`, stop maintaining it.

Zuckerberg won the argument and lost the week, and the chair's fact-check moved the target:
he says the funnel dies at handle claim; it dies one step earlier, at
`app/signin/page.tsx:31`, where `signIn('google', { redirectTo: '/me' })` is hardcoded with
no `callbackUrl` plumbing anywhere in the app — so Ola's invite context is destroyed before
she ever reaches the handle wall. The wall behind it is also taller than he stated: the
invite URL is `?a=<handle>&b=`, so the invitee must supply her own handle **and** flip
`isPublic`, both of which live only in `/settings`. Item 7 carries a validated same-origin
callback through sign-in; item 8 brings handle + public claim inline to `/compare`; item 9
is Cook's second auth path, which wins this gate instead if the events say the drop is at
Google itself. **No new prompt, banner, or share surface is built.** Four surfaces already
link into `/compare`; a fifth would be a wasted week on the wrong side of the loop, which is
the one thing Zuckerberg and the chair agree on completely.

### Gate 4 — Give them a reason to come back

**Entry:** unprompted weekly return is below 3 of 5 active members for four consecutive
rollups **while** activation is healthy — people activate and then vanish.
**Abandon:** unprompted weekly return is already ≥60% of active members (nothing to fix), or
≥30% of members opt out of the first two sends (the channel is unwelcome — kill it, do not
tune it).

`/api/next` already computes a personalised next skill and is consumed by
`MapHomeCard.tsx:32`; the recommender exists and only fires for people already in the door.
`vercel.json` contains only `{framework, regions}` — no crons — and no email package exists
outside the lock file, so today the product has zero ability to reach a member who does not
spontaneously visit. One weekly cron emailing each opted-in member their top pick is the
cheapest possible reason to return. It is deliberately not earlier: it costs the same email
infrastructure as Cook's magic link, and it presupposes the input metric it would be judged
on.

### Gate 5 — Raise or remove

**Entry:** `/ar/scan` and theme-gallery sessions run below 5% of skill-map sessions across
two consecutive rollups.
**Abandon:** their per-session engagement is at or above the skill map's — in which case
they are core, say so, and close the gate.

Cook's position, held in full: users do not average their experience, they remember the
weakest thing they touched. The core here is genuinely excellent — a token system with a
staleness test, 44px targets in both axes, a colour rule enforced by a custom ESLint rule,
sub-2ms layout — and two shipped surfaces sit inside the same product at a visibly lower
polish tier with 2 themes, 2 presets, and 1 share to show for a third of the engineering.
Raise each to the core's standard or take it out of the visible product. This gate is
*after* the acquisition and loop work, not before, on one ground only: removing a surface
nobody uses changes no metric, and Cook's own falsification test needs the instrumentation
from Gate 1 to run at all.

### Gate 6 — Content depth: Orchestras, as one screen

**Entry:** ≥3 users past 45 of 62 mastered, **or** the input metric shows the 62-skill map
being consumed faster than it can be extended. Today: one user at 59, average 21.4. Supply
exceeds demand by a wide margin.
**Abandon:** no licensed, importable orchestra corpus materialises — MusicBrainz tango
relations stay effectively empty (Di Sarli: one relation, no dates), tango.info keeps
returning 403 to bots with no declared licence, todotango stays unverified. In that world
Orchestras is one developer hand-authoring 13 entity types across ~60 orchestras with
singers still missing and three sections of the spec unwritten. That is a research and
writing project of unbounded length, not a screen, and it never gets built.

The working list at `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-07-orchestras-working-list.md` is a
genuinely good spec and costs nothing to leave committed. The rendering engine is largely
*done* and benchmarked (1.04ms layout at 250 tracks, 0.52ms columnar first paint) — the
weeks-eater is upstream, in the corpus, which is why the abandon condition is about data
licensing and not about engineering.

## 6. Where the panel still disagrees — kept visible

**The sharpest disagreement: what the binding constraint is at 7 users.**

- **Zuckerberg:** distribution *and the invite loop*. Fix the invitee side first; everything
  else is slower.
- **Bezos and Sarandos:** retention. Spinning a growth loop on a product whose average
  customer stalls at 21 of 62 is pouring water into a leaky bucket.
- **Pichai:** discoverability. The loop multiplies a population of two public profiles —
  approximately zero. There is no Ola to fix a bounce for.
- **Cook:** coherence. Nothing new ships until the weakest shipped thing is raised or
  removed.

**Ruling.** Bezos wins the definition of complete (§1). Pichai wins the nearest gate's
second item on a latency argument. Zuckerberg wins the *fact-check* — the prompt exists, the
consensus diagnosis was false, and no new prompt will be built — and loses the week, because
his own success criterion needs an existing user to send a WhatsApp invite and the
addressable sender pool is two people. Cook wins the constraint on *how* item 1 is built
(first-party only) and loses the sequencing. Sarandos wins the schema rider — per-skill
mastery timestamps — and loses the email.

**Two smaller tensions left standing, not smoothed over:**

1. **Gate 1 item 2 does not serve the chosen definition of complete.** SEO increases the
   input to the funnel; the definition measures the conversion within it. It is admitted on
   latency grounds alone and carries its own abandon condition at Gate 2. If that feels
   inconsistent, it is — and naming it is better than pretending the roadmap is one clean
   idea.
2. **The chosen definition of complete cannot detect a stranger.** Satisfying it proves the
   product works for five people the founder knows. That ceiling is real, it is why Gate 2
   exists, and the honest version of this roadmap says so rather than claiming Bezos's
   criterion is a proxy for product-market fit.
